Desk guide

How to make a market well

Aim for marked P&L, not just fills.

1. Quote two prices

Your bid is the price where you buy. Your ask is where you sell. The ask minus the bid is the bid-ask spread.

Spread is opportunity, not guaranteed profit. You earn it only if customer flow trades with you and later price moves do not overwhelm the position you carry.

2. Trade flow for risk

Tighten the spread when you are flat and want more chance of trading. Widen it when the market feels volatile or you already carry enough inventory.

Do not chase every fill. A wide quote may miss trades; a tight quote may fill quickly and leave you exposed to the next mark move.

3. Move with inventory

Long? Shift both prices down: a lower ask helps you sell, and a lower bid discourages more buying. Short? Shift both up: a higher bid helps you buy back, and a higher ask discourages more selling.

Flat? Keep a balanced quote around the reference mark. After every turn, use inventory, the mark, and coaching to decide whether to tighten, widen, or shift.
